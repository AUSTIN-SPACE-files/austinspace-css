The challenge

Seven hundred videos, no way in.

Priya's channel is built on "first time hearing" reactions, and requests are the lifeblood of it. With a catalogue past 700 videos, fans kept asking for songs she had already covered, and YouTube's own search was not good enough to answer the question "has she done this one?"

The brief was simple on paper: a search page, a way to request reactions through Ko-fi, and a link to Patreon for early access, with as little ongoing admin as possible because she uploads a lot.

The catalogue

  • More than 700 titles in inconsistent formats.
  • Artist and song reversed, typos, and covers and singing videos mixed in with the reactions.
  • Shorts that should not appear.

The hidden videos

  • Some reactions cannot stay on YouTube and live on Patreon instead.
  • Scheduled uploads should show as "coming soon" without exposing the video.

The platform

  • The page had to share a Squarespace site with a live photography business, without touching its styling, navigation or performance.
The solution

How it was built

Move the sync to her own account

The first quote assumed a live search against YouTube's public API. Two requests changed that: reactions that cannot stay on YouTube needed to point to Patreon, and scheduled uploads needed to appear as coming soon. A public API key cannot see private or scheduled videos, so it could never show either.

I moved the sync into a Google Apps Script bound to a Sheet Priya owns. She installed the hourly trigger herself, so it runs under her account and reads every upload, published or not. A channel check stops a run from the wrong Google account overwriting the catalogue. The scope grew, so I re-quoted before starting, and the price was fixed from that point.

Clean the catalogue before trusting it

More than 700 titles, written by hand over years, do not follow one format. Artist and song swap places, spellings drift, and covers and singing videos sit alongside the reactions. The parser tolerates typos and reversed order, filters out covers and singing videos, and detects Shorts by duration without spending an extra API call.

Before go-live, a dry run parsed the whole catalogue without writing anything. On the first pass 146 titles came back with no readable artist. Parser changes brought that to 29, and those 29 went into an Overrides tab by hand, where Priya can correct any future title the same way.

Publish nothing private

The page reads a small public feed that the script publishes, cached for five minutes, with private and scheduled video IDs removed before anything leaves her account. Search ignores case, accents and stray spaces, puts artists that start with the query first, and pages results thirty at a time.

Each result resolves to one of five states. An entry on the Exceptions tab outranks a live video, so a reaction moved to Patreon shows as unavailable on YouTube with a Patreon link. A song titled 1999 crashed search on its first test, because the title arrived as a number. The fix went into both the script and the page.

Fit it inside someone else's site

The reactions page shares a Squarespace site with Priya's photography business, which already sells packages and has its own styling. Everything lives in one Code Block, every class carries a pvr- prefix, and every style is scoped under one wrapper, so the photography pages are untouched and site-wide Code Injection is never used.

The theme pushed back in three places. Its button styles overrode mine until the selectors were made more specific. Its 24px root font made every rem value half as large again until I switched to pixels. Its cache delayed changes during testing. Five rounds of Priya's feedback went in before launch. One pagination fault surfaced afterwards and was fixed on 16 October.

What happens next

What Priya runs now

Fans can answer "has she reacted to this?" themselves in a couple of seconds, on any device. New uploads appear within the hour without her touching anything. The only manual jobs left are the ones that need her judgement: marking a request as taken, and flagging a reaction that has moved to Patreon. Both are a row in a spreadsheet.

How it was delivered

It launched on 30 September 2026. It was delivered at a fixed price within a creator's budget, and her expanded scope was absorbed without turning into an open-ended project. Usage figures will be added once there is enough data to report.

FAQ

Common questions

Can Squarespace search a YouTube channel?

Not natively. This build syncs the channel into a Google Sheet every hour with Apps Script, then a custom Code Block on the Squarespace page searches that feed. Visitors never leave the site.

Does a feature like this need ongoing maintenance?

Very little. New uploads sync automatically. The owner only edits the Sheet for things that need a human decision, such as marking a request as taken.

Can you add a custom feature without redesigning my whole Squarespace site?

Yes. This page shares a site with a separate photography business, and all its code and styling is scoped so the rest of the site is unaffected.

Do you work with YouTubers and creators?

Yes. Creator builds tend to be focused features rather than full sites, such as catalogue search, request flows and links to Patreon or Ko-fi, and they are priced to fit.

What happens when a reaction cannot stay on YouTube?

It stays searchable. A row in the Sheet marks it as an exception, and the result shows it as unavailable on YouTube with a link to watch it on Patreon instead.
