The challenge

What I inherited

A three page website built by someone else six years earlier, with a 2019 copyright still sitting in the footer and a broken logo image in the header on every page. The first thing any visitor saw was a placeholder icon where the company name should have been.

The bigger problem was what it said. All three services on offer were painting. Nothing about fire doors, building maintenance, specialist works, or the defence sector relationships that make up most of what DDR do. A procurement lead landing on it had no way of knowing whether the company could handle their job. In their own words, it was "very outdated and needs a complete freshen up".

Structure

  • Three pages total: home, case studies, contact
  • No services page and no about page
  • Still on the old trading domain the business had left
  • Two completed projects on a single shared page

Presentation

  • Header logo broken on every page
  • Three services listed, all of them painting
  • Stock photography throughout, none of it DDR's work
  • No certifications shown anywhere

Compliance

  • Two forms collecting name, email and phone
  • No privacy policy
  • No cookie policy
  • No consent mechanism of any kind
The solution

What I did about it

Started from their brief

Most clients arrive with nothing. DDR arrived with a slideshow covering the pages they wanted, the information to include and the projects to show. My job was to interrogate that, not replace it.

  • Worked through their content plan page by page before any design started
  • Agreed scope and a fixed price inside five days of first contact
  • Mapped content requirements per page so nothing stalled mid-build

Rebuilt around what they actually sell

The old site sold painting. The business does considerably more than that, and none of it was visible.

  • Ten pages plus policies, built from scratch rather than reskinned
  • Services split properly, with divisions given their own page
  • Detailed case studies for completed projects, each with its own page
  • Over ten certifications presented, none of which had appeared before
  • Structured enquiry form and named contacts replacing a basic email box
  • Review capture added so testimonials could keep accumulating

Moved the domain and closed the compliance gap

The business had already rebranded and bought the new domain before contacting me. What they had not done was move, and the old site had been collecting personal data with nothing in place to cover it.

  • Migrated from the old trading domain to the current one with redirects
  • Cookie consent, privacy policy and cookie policy implemented
  • Their new logo integrated across the site and the email signatures
  • SEO foundations applied: meta titles, descriptions, alt text, clean URLs, sitemap

Measured from launch, then they came back

Search Console was verified and the sitemap submitted on the day the site went live. Every number on this page exists because of that half hour. Eight months later DDR approved a second phase.

  • Analytics and Search Console configured at launch, not retrofitted
  • Admin access, a walkthrough and a written handover so the team could run it
  • Services page rebuilt from eight offerings to thirteen, custom coded
  • Accreditation logos brought into line with their branding guidelines
  • Custom global footer, and a new case study as projects completed
  • Two pieces of that second phase delivered free of charge

What the data does not say

In seven months the site earned zero clicks from non-brand searches. Every click came from someone who already knew the name.

That is not a failure of the build. DDR win work through framework relationships, not through people searching for contractors, and the number of people searching for defence sector refurbishment specialists is close to nothing. Building the site to chase search volume that does not exist would have been the wrong call.

I am saying it plainly because a case study claiming a traffic transformation here would be inventing one.

What it does instead

Anyone who hears about DDR through a framework, a site visit or a recommendation and goes looking now finds a current, credible site instead of a broken one on a dead domain. That is what the top five position for their own name is worth, and for this business it is the outcome that matters.

The site also does things the old one could not. It presents the certifications, it shows completed work, it captures enquiries and reviews, and it meets UK GDPR requirements on forms that previously had no policy behind them at all.

Measured from day one

Search Console and analytics were configured on launch day rather than months later. Every figure above comes from that, not from an estimate. It is a half hour of work at the right moment, and without it this section would be opinion.

FAQ

Common questions

Why does this case study not name the sites you worked on?

Because naming them helps nobody. The search data shows those pages pull hundreds of impressions from people looking for the locations themselves rather than for a contractor, and none of them convert. On a defence sector project the sensible default is to describe the work at sector level and leave the specifics to the client, who owns that decision and the relationship behind it.

The site does not rank for service terms. Is that not the point of SEO?

For most businesses, yes. For this one, no. DDR are appointed through frameworks, and the volume of people searching for defence sector refurbishment contractors is close to nothing. The site was built to be found by people who already had the name and to stand up to scrutiny when they arrived. It does both. Chasing search volume that does not exist would have been the wrong call, and I would rather say that than dress it up.

How long did it take?

Four weeks from the quote being accepted to the site being ready for launch, and five weeks from the first email. The client had their content largely prepared before we started, which is the single biggest factor in how quickly a build moves.

What happened after launch?

Nothing for eight months, then I got back in touch and the client approved further work the same day. That second phase rebuilt the services page, redid the footer, brought the accreditation logos into line with their branding guidelines and added a new case study. Two pieces of it were done free of charge, because the relationship is worth more than the hours.

Can you work on projects where the details are confidential?

Yes, and this is the example. Every claim on this page is evidenced, and everything that would identify a location, a contract or an individual has been left out. If your work involves clients who would rather not see their name on a supplier's marketing page, that is a constraint I am used to working inside.
